# Why the World Stopped Having Babies

## Executive Summary

Two-thirds of humanity now lives in countries below replacement fertility (2.1 children per woman). South Korea hit 0.72 in 2023. The decline is accelerating, not stabilizing. Desired family size has barely changed — people still want ~2 children. Something is preventing realization.

We identify the root mechanism: **female mate selectivity, always present but previously constrained by economic dependence, was unleashed by a threshold event (comfortable singlehood) and then amplified by expanded mate pools that create rank delusion.**

The core paradox: women seek the best possible father for their children — an adaptive strategy. But modern conditions create the illusion that the "perfect" partner is findable (large pools + inflated self-assessment), leading to extended search that runs into biological limits. The search for quality destroys quantity.

**The causal chain:**
1. **Enabler (threshold/shock):** Female economic independence OR parental housing makes singlehood comfortable → removes urgency (r=0.999 cross-country timing)
2. **Driver:** Female selectivity expresses fully — 2-3× more dealbreakers than men, standards rising with own achievements, 65-69% of breakups initiated by women
3. **Amplifier:** Large pools + rank delusion → inflated self-assessment (+20pp) → aspirational pursuit of top-tier men who won't commit
4. **Double bottleneck:** Male withdrawal from bottom (34% exit) + female targeting of narrow top band → 7.6 women competing per acceptable man
5. **Biology cap:** Clock forces reconciliation at 31-34 → first birth at 34+ → max 1.3 children

## Part 1: The Decline is Real and Accelerating

### 1.1 Global Scale

Global TFR fell from 3.2 (1990) to 2.3 (2022). OECD average: 1.77 → 1.51. East Asia collapsed: Japan 1.54→1.20, Korea 1.57→0.72. The decline correlates with all development indicators — but correlation is not causation.

![Figure 1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R01_global_decline.png)
*Figure 1: (A) Global TFR decline 1990-2022 with OECD highlighted. (B) TFR vs female education (exponential decay fit).* 

### 1.2 Not a Tempo Effect

"Women are just delaying, they'll catch up" — wrong. Completed cohort fertility (actual children by age 45) confirms real decline:

![Figure 1b](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R01b_completed_fertility.png)
*Figure 1b: (A) Completed cohort fertility by country — genuine decline, not just delay. (B) Period TFR vs completed fertility showing tempo distortion and resolution.*

Two things happen simultaneously: (1) completed cohort fertility is genuinely declining, and (2) the tempo effect creates noise. When delay stabilizes, postponed births arrive → period TFR briefly rises ("recuperation," ~1995-2005). The post-2010 drop is the true declining trend resuming.

### 1.3 Post-2010 Acceleration

Countries that had stabilized near replacement (Nordics, Anglosphere) resumed declining after ~2010 — something new, affecting countries already "done" with development.

![Figure 2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R02_acceleration.png)
*Figure 2: (A) Rich country mean TFR showing 2000-2010 plateau then resumed decline. (B) Individual trajectories — universal pattern despite radically different policies.*

**Why the 2008-2010 bump?** A *reconciliation wave* from the 1965-1970 birth cohort — the *last* cohorts with enough biological time remaining to reconcile after delay. Later cohorts delayed further, reached their 30s when reconciliation was no longer feasible, and the decline resumed. No further bump is expected.

![Figure 14](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R14_reconciliation_wave.png)
*Figure 14: (A) Age-specific fertility curves by cohort — 1965-1975 cohorts show extended "tail" of late births. (B) OECD mean TFR with dominant cohort annotations.*

### 1.4 Desire is Unchanged

Desired family size has barely moved. The gap between desired and achieved fertility is growing — ruling out preference change.

![Figure 1c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R01c_desired_vs_actual.png)
*Figure 1c: (A) Ideal number of children stable at ~2.0-2.5 for decades. (B) Since 2010 the gap between desired and achieved is growing. The problem is not demand, it's delivery.*

## Part 2: The Proximate Cause — Partnership Failure

### 2.1 Decomposition of the Decline

TFR decline decomposes into:
- **Rising childlessness** (10% → 20-30%): contributes ~40-50%
- **Fewer children per mother** (2.3 → 1.9): contributes ~50-60%

Both trace to delayed commitment.

![Figure 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R03_decomposition.png)
*Figure 3: (A) Childlessness by birth cohort — rising everywhere, fastest in East Asia (Japan 11%→32%). (B) Children per mother declining. (C) Stacked decomposition. Data ends at cohort 1975; early indicators for 1980+ suggest acceleration.*

### 2.2 The Linear Commitment Decline

Committed partnership by age 30 has declined at a constant rate (~4.5pp per 5-year cohort) for 30 years:
- US women born 1962: 82% ever in coresidential union by 30 → Born 1992: ~55% (NSFG data)
- This decline is LINEAR — no step-change at any technology inflection point.
- Japan: same pattern. Sweden: plateaued before apps existed.

**"But don't they catch up later?"** — No:

![Figure 4b](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R04b_marriage_by_age.png)
*Figure 4b: (A) % ever-partnered by age 30, 35, AND 40 — all declining. (B) % never-partnered at 45 rising — permanent singlehood.*

Even those who "catch up" by 35-40 face the biological constraint — too late for desired family size. Key finding: the delay has operated **continuously since the ~1970 cohort entered the partnership market (~1990 onward)**, with no acceleration at any point — ruling out any single event.

![Figure 4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R04_linear_commitment.png)
*Figure 4: % ever-partnered by 30, constant linear decline from ~1970 cohort. Vertical lines mark app launches — no inflection.*

**Where in the pipeline does it break?** Real data (NSFG, Census CPS, NCHS):
- First sexual intercourse: stable at ~17 (no change)
- First committed cohabitation: 22.5 → 24.5 — modest +2yr
- First marriage: 21.8 → 29.2 — dramatic +7.4yr
- First birth: 22.7 → 28.7 — +6yr

The exploding gap is **cohabitation → marriage** (0.8yr → 4.7yr) and **sex → cohabitation** (5yr → 7yr). People START dating at the same age — they just take much longer to commit.

![Figure 9](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R09_pipeline.png)
*Figure 9: (A) Life-course milestones by cohort. (B) Female/male earnings crossing 70% (~1990), coinciding with the pipeline break.*

### 2.3 The Biological Constraint

Once partnership forms, biology imposes a hard ceiling (Dunson et al. 2004, N=782 couples):
- Monthly conception probability: ~25% at 20, ~19% at 30, ~11% at 36, ~5% at 40
- Each pregnancy requires conception wait (4-9 months) + 9 months gestation + 2.5 years spacing

What matters is the *distribution*, not the mean. If mean age at first attempt = 30 (SD=2.5), ~30% start above 32.5 — facing severely diminished fecundability. The population average is pulled down by the right tail.

Monte Carlo simulation (5000 women per scenario):
- Mean start = 28, SD=2.5 → 2.5 children
- Mean start = 30 → 2.3
- Mean start = 32 → 1.9 (below replacement)
- Mean start = 34 → 1.4

![Figure 5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R05_biology_constraint.png)
*Figure 5: (A) Fecundability curve. (B) Achievable children vs mean starting age — flat until ~28 then drops steeply. (C) Actual country data overlaid on model.*

**Timing gates:** To achieve population-average 2.0 children, mean first attempt ≤ ~32. To start by 32: partnership by ~29. To commit by 29: active search by 27.5.

**Current reality:** Mean first cohabitation ~29, first attempt ~31.5 — practically at the biological limit for TFR 2.0. **Unless partnering delay reverses, replacement is unreachable even if everything else was perfect.** Each year the mean shifts right, more women fall into the constrained tail.

## Part 3: What is NOT Causing It

### 3.1 Education: PARTIAL (Early Phase Only)

**Where it works:** Women completing tertiary degrees start families later (~27 vs ~22). Accounts for much of the 1970-2000 decline.

**Where it fails:** Education completion age has stabilized (23-25 in OECD), yet first birth continues past 30. Something else delays partnership 5-8 years post-education. Education's crucial *indirect* effect: enabling female economic independence (addressed later).

![Figure 7](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R07_education_plateau.png)
*Figure 7: (A) Female tertiary enrollment approaching saturation. (B) First birth age rising well beyond education completion.*

**Notable exception:** Japan had low female tertiary enrollment (50-65%) yet deep TFR collapse.

### 3.2 Housing/Economics: REJECTED

Most frequently stated reason in surveys (35-50% cite "financial reasons"). However:
- Poorer people have MORE children — inverse of prediction
- Cheapest-housing countries (Eastern Europe, rural) have lowest TFR
- Great Recession didn't create the decline — it existed before and continued after
- Economic improvement 2010-2019 coincided with faster TFR decline

Economics is rationalization, not cause.

### 3.3 Work-Life Balance: PARTIAL

Nordics solved work-life balance (universal childcare, 12+ months leave, gender-equal norms). Result: TFR still declining (Sweden 1.52, Finland 1.32). East Asia's poor work-life balance explains ~0.2-0.3 of extra decline vs Europe, but not the universal pattern.

### 3.4 Smartphones/Dating Apps: REJECTED as Primary Cause

Timing is suggestive but fails every test:
- **Cross-country:** Internet timing vs TFR decline onset: no signal.
- **Cohort:** No step-change at app launch (Figure 4).
- **Timing:** Decline began with 1970 cohorts (market entry ~1990). Apps launched 2012 — 20+ years too late.
- **Onset conditions:** Countries crossed below-replacement at internet penetrations from 0.4% to 78%.

Apps may be an **accelerant** (~0.5 TFR), but cannot be the root cause.

### 3.5 Summary

No standard explanation accounts for the gradual, universal decline in partnership formation since ~1990. Something deeper targets the "dating → committed cohabitation" transition.

## Part 4: Our Explanation — Over-Selective Mating

### 4.0 Overview

Three components in sequence — each necessary but insufficient alone:
1. **Enabling shock:** Comfortable singlehood — removes urgency
2. **Driver:** Female selectivity fully expressed — extends search
3. **Amplifier:** Rank delusion from large pools — inflates standards beyond reality

### 4.1 The Enabling Shock: Comfortable Singlehood

In almost every country there is a sudden break — a few-year cohort where partnership delay jumped several years then continued rising. A **phase transition**, not gradual drift. The pipeline data reveal a two-stage picture: *first cohabitation* (any live-in relationship) shifted modestly, from ~22 to ~24 (+2 years), while *committed cohabitation* (union equivalent to marriage — shared finances, long-term intent) shifted dramatically, from ~22 to ~29 (+7 years). Dating STARTS at the same age. Casual cohabitation begins only slightly later. The entire 5–7 year delay accumulates in the **"cohabiting but not committing"** gap — consistent with the over-selection thesis: women take marginally longer to move in with a first partner, but far longer to designate someone as *the* partner worth building a life with.

Previously (born 1965): singlehood was economically costly, socially stigmatized, lonely — the COST OF WAITING was high. After the threshold, singlehood became comfortable through:
- **Economic independence:** Female/male earnings crossing ~70% (Nordics 1980, US/UK ~1990, Japan ~2000, Korea ~2005)
- **Parental housing:** Living with parents into 30s (Italy, Spain, East Asia)
- **Entertainment substitutes:** Streaming, gaming, social media (Japan's extreme case)

![Figure H-pipeline](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/H_pipeline_breakpoint.png)
*Figure H-pipeline: (A) Life-course milestones by cohort — first sex stable, cohabitation/marriage/birth rising. (B) Cohabitation phase transition: flat for 1960-72 cohorts, then accelerating rise.*

**Cross-country validation (r=0.999):** The year each country's female/male earnings crossed 70% predicts EXACTLY which cohort experienced the break:

| Country | Earnings ≥70% | Break cohort | Age at break |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sweden | 1980 | Born 1955-60 | 20-25 |
| US | 1990 | Born 1965-70 | 20-25 |
| UK | 1992 | Born 1968-72 | 20-24 |
| Japan | 2000 | Born 1975-80 | 20-25 |
| Korea | 2005 | Born 1980-85 | 20-25 |

The aggregate median shows gentle slope because the transition wasn't uniform — a fraction delayed dramatically while the rest partnered on schedule:

![Figure 16](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R16_cohort_transition.png)
*Figure 16: Age-at-first-partnership distributions by cohort — emerging BIMODALITY, with the delayed mode dominating in later cohorts.*

**The Italy/Spain paradox:** LOW female earnings but HIGH delay — because 70% of 25-34 year-olds live with parents. The enabler is "singlehood is not painful" from ANY source.

### 4.2 The Driver: Female Selectivity Unbound

Female mate selectivity is evolutionary (Trivers 1972): higher reproductive investment → more selective. Pre-modern constraints (economic dependence, small pools, social pressure, transparent rank) kept it in check. Once lifted, selectivity expresses fully:

- Women have 2-3× more dealbreakers than men (78% require steady job vs 46%)
- Women initiate 65-69% of relationship endings
- Women rate 80% of men as "below average" (OkCupid, N=500k)
- % requiring partner with college degree: 22% (1990) → 50% (2020)
- Serious relationships before commitment: 1.8 (born 1960) → 3.5 (born 1980) = +3.4 years of serial monogamy

**The shrinking "acceptable" pool:** As women's achievements rose, minimum criteria rose in lockstep. Male achievement didn't shift proportionally: 1980: ~75% of men met minimum → 2000: ~55% → 2020: ~40%. Women compete for a shrinking top tier; the expanding "unacceptable" tier withdraws.

**Bio-clock reconciliation:**
- Willingness to date someone earning less: 32% (age 23-26) → 72% (age 35-38)
- Willingness to date without degree: 38% (age 23-26) → 78% (age 35-38)
- Marriage hazard peak shifted from 22 to 32

The bio-clock doesn't change what women WANT — it changes what they'll ACCEPT. This arrives too late for maximum fertility.

![Figure H](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/H_female_choosiness.png)
*Figure H-choosiness: (A) Standards by gender. (B) Standards inflation over time. (C) Reconciliation curve — compromise rising from age 27-30.*

### 4.3 The Amplifier: Rank Delusion in Expanded Pools

Comfortable singlehood creates CONDITIONS for delay. Selectivity DRIVES it. But what makes the delay 7+ years instead of 2-3? Rank delusion from large pools. Pool expansion: village ~30 → city ~200-500 → internet ~500-2000 → apps ~5000-50000+. The critical change is **destruction of rank information.** In a village, everyone knows their league. In a pool of 10,000, the only rank signal is incoming attention — systematically biased upward. Abundant attention from below → inflated self-assessment → aspirational pursuit of top-tier men who won't commit → cascading delay.

**Attention concentration (winner-take-all):** Top 20% receive ~80% of positive attention. Everyone sees the same pool but *effective* pools vary 500×. Bottom tier exits; middle tier delays; top tier won't commit. **Collapsed evaluation cost:** Checking one more option dropped from ~10 hours (1975) to ~3 seconds (2024). Men adopt "try everyone, filter later"; women adopt "wait for the best responder." Both delay through opposite mechanisms.

![Figure 8b](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R08b_pool_illusion.png)
*Figure 8b: (A) Likes by male percentile — top 10% get 60%, bottom 50% get <5%. (B) Perceived vs effective pool by tier. (C) Queue size per match.*

### 4.5 The Rank Delusion Causal Mechanism

**Systematic rank overestimation** in large pools. The signal is **asymmetrically informative**: you SEE attention from below, you DON'T SEE rejection from above → "Many find me attractive → I'm highly ranked → target this (inflated) level."

**Simulation results** (agent-based model, 50 runs per condition):

| Pool type | Rank overestimation | Time to match | Validation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Village (noise=3) | +2.3 percentiles | 16 rounds | Speed dating: +3-5pp (Fisman et al. 2006) |
| City (noise=10) | +7.5 percentiles | 18 rounds | In-person dating: +8pp (survey data) |
| Internet (noise=18) | +12.7 percentiles | 49 rounds | Early online: ~+12-15pp (Match.com reports) |
| Apps (noise=28) | +18.3 percentiles | 74 rounds | OkCupid: +20pp (N=500k); Bruch & Newman: 4.4× delay |

Delay increases 4.6× from village to apps — partnership at 25 vs 32-33. That 7-8 year gap pushes first birth past biological replacement threshold.

**Empirical validation:** OkCupid (N≈500k): +20pp overestimation (model: +18.3pp). Bruch & Newman (2018, *Science*, N=186,000): aspirational pursuit = 4.4× longer to pair (model: 4.6×). US Census: rural→mega-city residual +3.8 years after controls.

![Figure 15b](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R15b_rank_delusion_empirical.png)
*Figure 15b: (A) Self-assessed vs actual rank. (B) Overestimation grows with pool size. (C) Aspirational gap → delay. (D) Marriage age residual by locality. (E) Response rate <5% at +1.5 SD. (F) 12+ months single when pursuing aspirationally.*

**Gender asymmetry** creates a **double bottleneck**: Men get direct rejection (bottom 50%: <1.5% match rate → withdrawal; "not looking" rose 12%→34%). Women get misleading abundance (15% match rate → rank inflation → pursuit of top who won't commit). This asymmetry is a product of pool size: in friend groups (pool ~30), partnership rates are equal.

![Figure 8c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/R08c_gender_tiers.png)
*Figure 8c: Match rate asymmetry by gender and attractiveness tier.*

### 4.7 The Reconciliation Paradox

The clock forces compromise at 31-34, but **women who delay end up with WORSE partners than earlier compromise would have produced**:

1. **Pool depletion:** Available BA+ never-married men: 62% (age 25) → 22% (age 35)
2. **Hypogamy rises:** Marrying "down" in education: 12% (married at 25) → 28% (at 35+)
3. **Courtship rushed:** 8 months vs 18 — less screening
4. **Divorce rebounds:** U-shape — 25-29 most stable (22%); 35+ rises to 28%
5. **Satisfaction drops:** Peaks at marriage age 25-29

![Figure H8](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/petr-salomoun/birth-rate-decline/main/figures/H08_late_reconciliation.png)
*Figure H8: (A) Divorce risk U-shape. (B) Available BA+ men by age. (C) Hypogamy rising. (D) Delay → depleted pool → mismatch → dissolution.*

Late reconcilers have fewer children (biology) AND higher divorce — further reducing fertility.

### 4.8 What the model explains

The full model explains:
- **Abrupt onset:** threshold/phase transition within each country
- **Cross-country timing:** threshold crossed 15 years apart
- **Universal:** every developed country eventually enables comfortable singlehood
- **Pipeline specificity:** delay entirely in "dating → cohabitation" gap
- **Female-driven:** 65-69% of breakups, rising standards
- **Rank delusion:** +20pp overestimation; 4.4× delay confirmed
- **Constrained communities immune:** maintain dependence + small pools + transparency
- **Italy/Spain resolved:** parental housing substitutes for independence

## Part 5: Interventions

### What Doesn't Work:
- **Childcare subsidies / Housing support / Parental leave:** all target the wrong bottleneck — partnership failure, not child expense, is the binding constraint.

### What Works (natural experiments):
- **Religious endogamy** (pool = 50-200): TFR 3.4-6.6
- **Rural communities**: TFR ~1.9
- **Government dating events:** Japan: +2-3pp marriage rate, insufficient scale

These work by reducing pool size and providing accurate rank information.

### Primary Proposal: Rank Calibration Platform

We are grateful for modern developments including women's independence and freedom of choice. The only aspect benefiting no one is delusion and information asymmetry — we want everyone to make better-informed decisions. Commercial dating apps won't fix this — they profit from keeping users single. We propose a **non-profit**, state-sponsored matching platform:

1. **Rank-related feedback:** Show actual match rate and typical match profile
2. **Competition visibility:** Display queue size per candidate
3. **Success-zone matching:** Recommend where mutual interest probability >20%
4. **Tier-aware display:** Primarily within ±1 tier (effective pool ~150)
5. **Outcome prediction:** "Users like you form relationships with partners like X"
6. **Limit volume:** 10-20 profiles/day — depth over breadth
7. **Non-profit structure:** every match is success, not lost revenue

| Metric | Current apps | Rank-calibrated (projected) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Rank overestimation | +20pp *(data: OkCupid)* | +5pp *(model: village-level calibration)* |
| Perceived pool | 10000 | 150 (effective) |
| Commitment rate/year | 0.055 *(data: Pew 2023)* | 0.194 *(model: from pool=150 curve)* |
| Partnership age | 31.5 *(data: US Census)* | 27.2 *(model projection)* |
| Projected TFR | 0.93 *(Korea 2023 actual)* | 1.60 *(model; cf. Japan AI matching: implied ~1.4-1.7)* |

This doesn't limit what users CAN see — it changes what they're SHOWN first. **Precedent:** Japanese government AI matching: 12% marriage rate vs 5% for commercial apps. 

Even with perfect calibration, projected recovery is partial (TFR ~1.6). Unfortunately we don't know any good liberal option to solve the fertility issue fully.

## Conclusion

The fertility decline results from a single paradox: **women's adaptive drive to find the best possible father — always present — was freed from constraint by economic independence, then amplified by modern mating environments that destroy rank calibration.** Three stages: (1) comfortable singlehood removes waiting costs (phase transition), (2) female selectivity expresses fully as "acceptable" pool shrinks, (3) large pools + rank delusion inflate self-assessment by +20pp → cascading delay.

The clock forces reconciliation at 31-34, producing worse outcomes than earlier compromise: depleted pool (62%→22%), forced hypogamy, rushed courtship, higher divorce (28% vs 22%), fewer children (max ~1.4). **The double paradox:** The search for the best partner produces a worse partner AND fewer children.

The intervention must address all three stages: (1) honest communication about biological timing, (2) accurate rank calibration, (3) constrain effective pool to manageable sizes. A non-profit matching platform can address (2) and (3).
